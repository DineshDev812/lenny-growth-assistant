# Ship 30 for 30 Essay Skill

## Purpose
Transforms retrieved transcript evidence into an engaging, high-retention essay formatted according to Dickie Bush & Nicolas Cole's **Ship 30 for 30** writing methodology, while strictly citing source material from Lenny's Podcast.

## Writing Principles (from Ship 30 for 30)

1. **The Hook (Headline + First 3 Lines)**:
   - Make a clear, irresistible promise.
   - State the problem, the counter-intuitive insight, and who this is for.
   - Use the 1-3-1 rhythm: one short opening line, three supporting sentences, one punchy transition line.

2. **Rate of Revelation**:
   - Deliver new value in every section. No fluff, no filler adjectives, no throat-clearing intro.
   - Speed up the reader's "aha!" moments.

3. **Formatting for Scannability & Rhythm**:
   - **One idea per sentence**; keep paragraphs under 3 lines.
   - Use numbered action headings (e.g. `### 1. Shift from Tactical to Strategic Retention`).
   - Bulleted lists for frameworks and rules of thumb.
   - **Selective Bolding**: Bold power phrases and crucial conclusions to guide the skimming reader.

4. **Narrative Progression**:
   - *Phase 1: The Trap* — What 90% of product and growth teams get wrong.
   - *Phase 2: The Core Framework* — The counter-intuitive model revealed by the expert guests.
   - *Phase 3: Tactical Implementation* — Step-by-step playbooks backed by transcript evidence.
   - *Phase 4: The Golden Takeaway* — A memorable summary rule the reader can apply tomorrow.

5. **Strict Evidence Grounding & Citations**:
   - All tactics, anecdotes, metrics, and quotes must come from the retrieved transcript chunks.
   - Cite every factual claim using bracketed sources: `[S1]`, `[S2]`.
   - Never extrapolate beyond what the guests explicitly shared.
